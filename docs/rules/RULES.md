# Rules
Edit only via finance-reflect. Format: `- ID: rule — why`. No personal amounts, accounts or credentials.

## Finances

- FIN1: Use the agreed reconciliation as the starting ledger and preserve reviewed decisions across overlapping imports — repeated statements must not duplicate expenses or undo review.
- FIN2: Split confirmed shared household and all cat expenses equally, regardless of payer or pet ownership — ownership does not determine the agreed share.
- FIN3: Keep rent repayment agreements, ongoing rent, everyday expenses and reimbursements distinct — their balances and payment timing differ.
- FIN4: Exclude refundable deposits and already-settled balances from new shared-expense charges — neither creates a fresh living expense.
- FIN5: Default personal budgets to private expenses plus the person's half of shared expenses; show actual cash movement separately — payment and economic responsibility differ.
- FIN6: Support flexible budget periods, defaulting to the 11th through the following 10th — budget periods must remain independent of statement coverage.
- FIN7: Support editable expense and income categories, subcategories and category budgets — users need to filter activity and compare targets with actuals.
- FIN8: Request clarification for ambiguous income components and shared classifications — missing information must not become an invented allocation.
- FIN9: Offer optional suggestions for historical corrections while preserving traceable accounting — declining advice must not break normal operation.

## App

- APP1: Keep the database local and sensitive financial data out of application code and remote repositories; store no access keys, government IDs, card or bank-account identifiers — protect personal information.
- APP2: Provide free local protection without subscriptions or separately required applications — protection must remain independently usable.
- APP3: Open on the shared-household dashboard and provide separate personal dashboards for both partners — support both joint and individual financial management.
- APP4: Support both partners' bank and card statements through modular issuer adapters, including overlapping annual and irregular periods — institutions and statement boundaries vary.
- APP5: Start with bank/card activity and budgets, leaving practical extension points for savings, investments, pensions and debts — enable expansion without speculative overengineering.
- APP6: Separate desktop presentation from financial logic and persistence; use English UI with Hebrew text support and Israeli context — layout changes must not require accounting changes.
- APP7: Verify current official Israeli rules when implementing features that depend on law or regulation — do not rely on stale assumptions.
- APP8: Preserve record integrity through stable identities, audited changes, tested migrations, recovery and financial regression checks — routine fixes must not damage existing entries.
- APP9: Keep implementation changes narrowly scoped and improve relevant extension boundaries when touched — maintainability must not become unrelated refactoring.
